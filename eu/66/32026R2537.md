# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2537 of 17 November 2026 establishing the standard data communication form in accordance with Article 29 of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 of the European Parliament and of the Council

|  |  |  |

|  | 2026/2537 | 18.11.2026 |

THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Directive (EU) 2019/1023 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 June 2019 on preventive restructuring frameworks, on discharge of debt and disqualifications, and on measures to increase the efficiency of procedures concerning restructuring, insolvency and discharge of debt, and amending Directive (EU) 2017/1132 (Directive on restructuring and insolvency) [^ntr1-L_202602537EN.000101-E0001], and in particular Article 29(7) thereof,

Whereas:

- (1) To collect reliable and comparable data on insolvency and restructuring procedures and discharge of debt procedures a standard communication form should be created. As different Member States have different types of procedures, that form should help ensure that the data collected is comparable and easy to understand so that it can be published in an accessible and user-friendly manner.

- (2) The standard communication form should ensure that data is provided in a structured manner, differentiated according to types of insolvency, restructuring and debt discharge procedures while ensuring a sufficient degree of similarity in order to account for different types of such procedures existing in Member States.

- (3) The data on insolvency, restructuring and debt discharge procedures should be collected by Member States on the basis of full aggregation or sample-based aggregation and communicated to the Commission electronically.

- (4) To achieve the necessary level of comparability of data on the length of insolvency, restructuring and debt discharge procedures, explanations should be provided by Member States as to the moment of the beginning and end of different types of procedures. For the same purpose, types of outcomes of insolvency, restructuring and debt discharge procedures should be defined as well as details specifying elements of costs of procedures, the number of restarts of new businesses and the jobs lost in relation to those procedures.

- (5) Since different types of data should be collected and provided in respect of different types of enterprises and natural persons, it is necessary to define those types of enterprises and natural persons.

- (6) Member States that choose to communicate data to the Commission on the basis of sample-based aggregation, should also provide data about the size and diversity aspects of the method of sample-based data aggregation.

- (7) In some Member States the communication may consider certain acts or procedures that appear as an integral part of insolvency procedures in the meaning of this Regulation, but do not constitute a self-standing procedure or the opening phase of a procedure, as ‘sub-procedures’. A debt discharge procedure should not be deemed to be a sub-procedure merely because it is an integral part of the insolvency proceedings under national insolvency law as long as the Member State collects and provides the data of statistical relevance on the discharge procedure.

- (8) The measures provided for in this Regulation are in accordance with the opinion of the committee referred to in Article 30 of Directive (EU) 2019/1023,

HAS ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:

#### Article 1

1. The standard data communication form referred to in Article 29(6) of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 shall be as set out in Annex I to this Regulation.

2. The standard data communication form shall be filled in accordance with the instructions set out in Annex II.

#### Article 2

For the purposes of this Regulation, the following definitions apply:

- (1) ‘reference year’ means the full calendar year ending on 31 December;

- (2) ‘reporting year’ means the year in which the data referred to in Article 29 of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 are to be communicated to the Commission;

- (3) ‘full aggregation’ means a data collection method under which data for all insolvency procedures, restructuring procedures and discharge of debt procedures from all courts or other authorities competent for insolvency or restructuring procedures in the Member State are collected;

- (4) ‘sample-based aggregation’ means a data collection method under which data for all insolvency procedures, restructuring procedures and discharge of debt procedures from less than all courts or other authorities competent for insolvency or restructuring procedures in the Member State are collected;

- (5) ‘insolvency restructuring procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State involving restructuring:

- (a) the principal object of which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can only be the restructuring of the debtor; and

- (b) during the whole course of which the debtor is insolvent;

- (6) ‘insolvency liquidation procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State involving insolvency liquidation:

- (a) which is instituted with the aim of winding up of the debtor’s business and the principal object of which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can only be one of the following:

- (i) liquidation of the debtor’s assets;

- (ii) in case of absence of assets, the termination of the debtor’s business activities;

and

- (b) during the whole course of which the debtor is insolvent;

- (7) ‘general insolvency procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State other than that referred to in points (5) and (6):

- (a) which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can have one or more of the following objects as its principal object:

- (i) liquidation of the debtor’s assets;

- (ii) restructuring of the debtor;

- (iii) in case of absence of assets, the termination of the debtor’s business activities;

and

- (b) during the whole course of which:

- (i) its principal object can change anytime without conversion of such procedure under national law into a procedure of a different type referred to in points (5), (6) and (8) to (12); and

- (ii) the debtor is insolvent;

- (8) ‘pre-insolvency restructuring procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State involving restructuring:

- (a) the principal object of which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can only be restructuring of the debtor; and

- (b) during the whole course of which the debtor is in the likelihood of insolvency;

- (9) ‘general restructuring procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State involving restructuring:

- (a) the principal object of which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can only be the restructuring of the debtor; and

- (b) during the course of which one of the following applies:

- (i) the debtor can be in the likelihood of insolvency or insolvent or in another situation of financial difficulty;

- (ii) it is not verified by the court or other competent authority whether the debtor is insolvent or in the likelihood of insolvency or in another situation of financial difficulty;

- (10) ‘other insolvency procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State other than that referred to under points (5) to (9) and (11):

- (a) which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination can have one or more of the following objects as its principal object:

- (i) liquidation of the debtor’s assets which is instituted with the aim of winding up of the debtor’s business;

- (ii) restructuring of the debtor;

- (iii) in case of absence of assets, the termination of the debtor’s business activities;

and

- (b) during the course of which its principal object can change anytime without conversion of such procedure under national law into a procedure of a different type referred to in points (5) to (9) and (11) to (12), and one of the following applies:

- (i) the debtor can be in the likelihood of insolvency or insolvent or in other situation of financial difficulty;

- (ii) it is not verified by the court or other competent authority whether the debtor is insolvent or in the likelihood of insolvency or in other situation of financial difficulty;

- (11) ‘related insolvency or restructuring procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State other than that referred to in points (5) to (10):

- (a) which, from the moment when it is applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under the law of a Member State, until its termination, can have as its principal object one or more of the following insolvency or restructuring related issues:

- (i) preliminary acts performed for the purpose of establishing the fulfilment of conditions for a start of the procedure concerned, including the verification of conditions of eligibility of the debtor for any procedure referred to in points (5) to (10) and (12);

- (ii) granting a temporary stay of individual enforcement proceedings;

- (iii) involuntary dissolution of the legal personality of a legal person directly triggered by insolvency of such a legal person, unless such involuntary dissolution is a result of a declaration of inadmissibility, rejection or withdrawal of an application for insolvency;

and

- (b) during the course of which one of the following applies:

- (i) the debtor can be in the likelihood of insolvency or insolvent or in another situation of financial difficulty;

- (ii) it is not verified by the court or other competent authority whether the debtor is insolvent or in the likelihood of insolvency or in a situation of financial difficulty;

- (12) ‘debt discharge procedure’ means a procedure under the law of a Member State which has as its principal object the discharge or cancellation of debts of an insolvent debtor which is a natural person;

- (13) ‘self-standing procedure’ means a procedure defined in points (5) to (12) in respect of which a Member State can separately collect data in accordance with Article 29(6) of Directive (EU) 2019/1023;

- (14) ‘sub-procedure’ means a procedure which as an integral part from the data collection point of view of any of the procedures defined in points (5) to (11) does not constitute a self-standing procedure or the opening phase of a procedure and which may include:

- (a) a debt discharge procedure;

- (b) any of the acts referred to in point 11(a).

#### Article 3

1. The data shall be collected by one of the following methods:

- (a) full aggregation;

- (b) sample-based aggregation.

2. The data collected shall be provided to the Commission via an electronic application developed by the Commission. The Commission shall make available to Member States the access to that application.

3. Member States shall communicate the data in one of the official languages of the Union.

4. Insolvency procedures of Member States listed in Annex A to Regulation (EU) 2015/848 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr2-L_202602537EN.000101-E0002] shall be communicated among the procedures falling under the relevant types of procedures defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), of this Regulation. Member States need not distinguish between public and non-public procedures.

5. In relation to procedures defined in Article 2, points (8) to (11), Member States shall indicate which of those procedures contain an element or elements which implement Title II of Directive (EU) 2019/1023.

#### Article 4

1. For the purposes of Article 29(1), point (b), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, Member States shall indicate an overall period of time expressed in days for which the insolvency procedures, restructuring procedures or discharge of debt procedures have lasted in the Member State.

2. When communicating the average length of procedures Member States shall provide:

- (a) information on the average length of procedures until the moment of their closure;

- (b) explanations as to:

- (i) the start of the procedure consisting of an explanation when:

- (1) the moment of submission of the application occurs;

- (2) the moment of opening of the procedure, where such opening is provided for under national law, occurs;

- (ii) inclusion of any sub-procedure within the self-standing procedure with the indication of average length of such sub-procedure and average length of the remaining part of the self-standing procedure and whether the average length of any sub-procedure was counted as an estimated fraction or estimated percentage of the average time of the self-standing procedure of which the respective sub-procedure is an integral part accompanied by a justification of the amount of such fraction or percentage;

- (iii) closure of the procedure consisting of explanations as to when the moment of the end of the procedure occurs.

#### Article 5

1. For the purposes of Article 29(1), point (c), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, the types of outcomes shall include with respect to:

- (a) insolvency liquidation procedures, general insolvency procedures, other insolvency procedures and related insolvency or restructuring procedures, one or more of the following outcomes:

- (i) sale of assets, including sale of the debtor’s business or part thereof as a going concern, such as a sale of such business or part thereof as a productive unit or operating economic entity;

- (ii) discontinuation of the procedure, in particular due to a lack of assets to cover the costs of such procedure, without resolution of the legal relationships concerning an asset-less estate;

- (iii) closure of the procedure after resolution of the legal relationships concerning an asset-less estate;

- (iv) where relevant, discharge of debt of natural persons, where the debt discharge procedure is a sub-procedure of a self-standing procedure referred in this point, with the indication of the outcome referred to in point (c), if available;

- (v) any of the outcomes referred to in point (b)(i), (ii) and (iii);

- (vi) any other type of outcome under national law;

- (b) insolvency restructuring procedures, pre-insolvency restructuring procedures, general restructuring procedures, one or more of the following outcomes:

- (i) a plan being confirmed by a court;

- (ii) a plan not being confirmed by a court while the restructuring procedure is converted into an insolvency procedure referred to in Article 2 of this Regulation or closed;

- (iii) a plan not being confirmed by a court while another type of continuation than the one referred to in point (ii) takes place, including but not limited to any type of outcome referred to in point (a);

- (iv) where relevant, one or more outcomes referred to in point (c), if the debt discharge procedure is a sub-procedure of a self-standing procedure referred to in point (b);

- (v) another type of outcome under national law, which may include but is not limited to outcomes referred to in point (a);

- (c) debt discharge procedure, including where the debt discharge procedure is a sub-procedure of a self-standing procedure referred to in points (a) and (b), one or more of the following outcomes:

- (i) the discharge period elapsed without being extended or the discharge of debt was confirmed after the lapse of the discharge period;

- (ii) the discharge period was extended;

- (iii) the discharge of debt was revoked;

- (iv) the discharge of debt was denied;

- (v) confirmation of the repayment plan;

- (vi) absence of confirmation of the repayment plan;

- (vii) another type of outcome under national law, which may include but is not limited to one or more outcomes referred to in points (a) or (b), in particular if the debt discharge procedure was as a sub-procedure a part of a self-standing procedure referred to under any of those points;

- (d) restructurings which were performed outside of court or other authority competent for insolvency or restructuring procedures, the outcome is the act of registration with those courts or other authorities competent for insolvency without triggering before them any of the procedures defined in Article 2, points (5), (7), (8), (9), (10) or (11);

- (e) appeals against the outcome of procedures defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), where Member States report on these, one of the following outcomes:

- (i) lodgement of the appeal;

- (ii) declaration of inadmissibility or rejection of the appeal or its withdrawal before the appeal proceedings were opened;

- (iii) upholding of the appeal by the relevant court or authority.

2. Member States, when communicating data on outcomes, may in addition break down the number of individual types of outcomes in relation to types of procedures which exist under their law which are more specific than procedural types defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), if they decide also to provide data on these more specific types of procedures which exist under their law.

#### Article 6

1. For the purposes of Article 29(3), point (a), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, if Member States collect and aggregate data referred to in that point, they shall indicate the average cost of each type of procedure related to the respective insolvency or restructuring procedure or debt discharge procedure defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), expressed as an absolute number or in percentage, counted on the basis of the full aggregation method or sample-based aggregation method as a ratio:

- (a) of the costs of the procedure laid down in the decision or other document by which the outcome of the respective procedure is made or a related separate decision or other document where such costs are indicated;

- (b) to the overall value of the debtor’s estate determined at the beginning of the procedure or throughout its course.

The amount from which the average cost of the procedure is counted shall always include the cost of intervention of the insolvency practitioner, and, if laid down in one of the decisions or other documents referred to in the first subparagraph, point (a), it may also involve court fees, government levies or other related costs.

2. For the purposes of Article 29(3), point (b), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, if Member States collect and aggregate data referred to in that point, they shall indicate an overall amount of disbursement to creditors made in the respective insolvency or restructuring procedure or debt discharge procedure defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), expressed in percentage, counted on the basis of the full aggregation method or sample-based aggregation method as a ratio:

- (a) of the actual value of disbursed creditors’ claims laid down in the decision or other document by which the outcome of the respective procedure is made final in the first instance;

- (b) to the value of the same claims filed into the respective procedure by any type of creditor referred to in Article 29(3), point (b), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 in respect of such type of a creditor.

The average recovery rate indicated may be given in respect of different types of creditors such as secured creditors, unsecured creditors, and where applicable, other types of creditors.

3. For the purposes of Article 29(3), point (c), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, if Member States collect and aggregate data referred to in that point, they shall indicate the number of entrepreneurs who within 12 months after the closure of a procedure under Article 1(1), point (b), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 started a new business.

4. For the purposes of Article 29(3), point (d), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023, if Member States collect and aggregate data referred to in that point, they shall indicate the total amount of jobs lost in the reporting Member State within the reference year counted on the basis of the full aggregation method or sample-based aggregation method as the number of all jobs lost in the Member State in all enterprises which entered into any procedure within each type of procedure defined in Article 2, points (5) to (11) of this Regulation.

5. The number of jobs lost linked to the respective restructuring or insolvency procedure defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), can be counted as the difference between the number of jobs within the enterprise at the moment when such respective insolvency or restructuring procedure was applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under national law, and the number of jobs maintained at the moment of closure of such insolvency or restructuring procedure.

#### Article 7

1. For the purposes of Article 29(4), point (a), of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 the following categories shall be used for the size of the debtors that are not natural persons:

- (a) enterprises with less than 10 employees;

- (b) enterprises with between 10 and 249 employees;

- (c) enterprises with 250 employees or more;

- (d) unknown number of employees, if relevant.

The number of employees shall be expressed in annual work units in Member States which apply Article 5 of Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC [^ntr3-L_202602537EN.000101-E0003] in the context of insolvency statistics. Member States that do not apply Article 5 of that Recommendation may express the number of employees in headcount. Member States shall inform the Commission about the methodology they used to calculate the number of employees.

2. The size of debtors shall be indicated under one of the categories listed in the first paragraph:

- (a) either as to the date of the end of the last accounting period covered by the last published annual financial statement of the enterprise provided that the information on the number of employees is contained therein;

- (b) alternatively on the basis of other documents communicated to the court or other authority competent for the insolvency or restructuring proceedings before or at the start of the relevant procedure.

3. In respect of procedures leading to a discharge of debt, including in the form of sub-procedures, Member States shall indicate which of the following types of natural persons those procedures concern:

- (a) entrepreneurs and other natural persons than entrepreneurs;

- (b) entrepreneurs only;

- (c) other natural persons than entrepreneurs only.

#### Article 8

1. In the case of sample-based aggregation data collection, the minimum sample requirement in terms of size referred to in Article 29(5) of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 shall consist of at least 5 % of the total number of each type of the procedures defined in Article 2, points (5) to (12), of this Regulation, which existed in the Member States in the reference year and which:

- (a) were applied for or opened, where such opening is provided for under national law, and which were pending;

- (b) were closed; or

- (c) were declared inadmissible, rejected or withdrawn before being opened.

2. Minimum sample requirements in terms of diversity referred to in Article 29(5) of Directive (EU) 2019/1023 shall consist of at least 5 % of the total number of courts and other authorities competent for the administration of each type of the procedures referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article.

#### Article 9

The first reference year shall be the year 2027.

Member States need not communicate procedures that were initiated before 17 July 2022.

#### Article 10

This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the *Official Journal of the European Union*.

This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.
