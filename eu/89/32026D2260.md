# Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2260 of 8 October 2026 laying down rules for the application of Directive 2010/75/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards uniform conditions for operating rules for rearing of poultry and pigs (notified under document C(2026) 6899)

THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Directive 2010/75/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 November 2010 on industrial and livestock rearing emissions (integrated pollution prevention and control) [^ntr1-L_202602260EN.000101-E0001] , and in particular Article 70i(2), second subparagraph, thereof,

Whereas:

- (1) Directive 2010/75/EU lays down rules to prevent or reduce pollution arising from industrial activities. In this context, the Commission is to establish uniform conditions for operating rules for the activities referred to in Annex Ia to that Directive, namely the rearing of pigs and poultry of a certain capacity.

- (2) A sub-group to the Industrial Emissions Expert Group has been created to support the Commission in the early preparation of the implementing act establishing the uniform conditions for operating rules for poultry and pigs rearing in accordance with Article 70i IED, before submission to the committee in accordance with Regulation (EU) No 182/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr2-L_202602260EN.000101-E0002]. The sub-group fulfils the call on the Commission, pursuant to Article 70i(1) of the IED to carry out appropriate consultations and organise an exchange of information during the preparatory work of these uniform conditions, including at expert level.

- (3) The sub-group met on several occasions from February 2025 until May 2026. In this context, measures concerning on-farm processes and activities, such as nutritional management, feed preparation, housing, manure management, including collection, storage, processing and land spreading, as well as storage of dead animals were discussed. The sub-group provided data and shared information, based on which the uniform conditions for operating rules have been developed.

- (4) The economic feasibility of certain techniques varies depending on the farm size. Consequently, it is appropriate to set proportionate requirements for farms carrying out activities referred to in Annex Ia to Directive 2010/75/EU which did not fall within the scope of that Directive before 4 August 2024.

- (5) In order to allow for the most cost-effective techniques to be employed at farm level, the best-available techniques and emerging techniques referred to in the uniform conditions for operating rules should be neither prescriptive nor exhaustive. It should be possible to use other techniques that ensure at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, including human health and climate protection. Therefore, in the event that techniques not listed in the uniform conditions are applied, it is necessary that Member States validate that their use ensures at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, based on scientifically established methods, also taking into account animal welfare systems recognised at Union, national or international level.

- (6) Information on the environmental performance of the techniques listed in the uniform conditions for operating rules (e.g. efficiency for ammonia emissions reduction) is based on the information exchange pursuant to Article 70i(1) of Directive 2010/75/EU, and on recognised international guidance (e.g. the UNECE guidance document on ‘Options for ammonia mitigation’ [^ntr3-L_202602260EN.000101-E0003]). It should be possible to use additional information on the environmental performance of the techniques from Union, national or international sources that ensure the provision of data of an equivalent scientific quality. When the use of additional information leads to different environmental performance than the one listed in the uniform conditions, or when the environmental performance of the techniques is not specified in the uniform conditions, it is necessary that Member States, as appropriate, validate the environmental performance of the techniques, based on scientifically established methods.

- (7) It is also necessary that Member States validate the environmental performance of combinations of techniques, based on scientifically established methods, and that Member States ensure that all underlying data, methodologies, and assumptions are fully documented and publicly available.

- (8) To reduce the administrative burden involved in the application of techniques not listed in the uniform conditions for operating rules, Member States should strive to cooperate in order to foster mutual recognition of techniques.

- (9) The maximum values of ammonia emissions to air established in these uniform conditions for operating rules should be understood as setting emission limit values as referred to in Article 70d(2) and Article 70f(1) of the directive.

- (10) The maximum values of total nitrogen excreted and maximum values of total phosphorus excreted established in the uniform conditions for operating rules should be understood as setting environmental performance limit values as referred to in Article 70d(2) and Article 70f(1) of Directive 2010/75/EU.

- (11) Article 3(5) of Directive (EU) 2024/1785 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr4-L_202602260EN.000101-E0004] sets out that in relation to installations carrying out activities referred to in Annex Ia to Directive 2010/75/EU, Member States are required to apply the laws, regulations and administrative provisions adopted in accordance with Directive (EU) 2024/1785 within: (a) four years of the entry into force of the implementing act referred to in Article 70i(2) of Directive 2010/75/EU, if the installation has a capacity of 600 livestock units (LSU) or more; (b) five years of the entry into force of the implementing act referred to in Article 70i(2) of Directive 2010/75/EU, if the installation has a capacity of 400 LSU or more; (c) six years of the entry into force of the implementing act referred to in Article 70i(2) of Directive 2010/75/EU, for all other installations covered by Annex Ia to Directive 2010/75/EU. Until the relevant date of application, as referred to in Article 3(5), first subparagraph, of Directive (EU) 2024/1785, the installations mentioned in that subparagraph which fall within the scope of Directive 2010/75/EU in the version in force on the day before Directive (EU) 2024/1785 enters into force are required to comply with Directive 2010/75/EU in that version. It is therefore appropriate, for the purpose of applying the uniform conditions for operating rules, that a ‘new farm’ is defined as a farm whose construction has been authorised or registered after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of Directive (EU) 2024/1785, or as a complete replacement of a farm after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of Directive (EU) 2024/1785.

- (12) The maximum values of ammonia emissions to air from animal housing should be set at the level of each animal house present at the farm. However, Member States should be given the option to apply maximum values across all animal houses at the same farm, provided that there is no net increase in ammonia emissions from housing in the farm, as this would enable the implementation of cost-effective measures by farmers.

- (13) As an alternative to the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air set out in the uniform conditions, Member States should be allowed to use a relative system based on minimum ammonia reduction requirements at each animal house as compared to a reference system based on Union or international sources (e.g. UNECE guidance document on ‘Options for ammonia mitigation’ [^ntr5-L_202602260EN.000101-E0005]), provided that it ensures at least the same reduction of ammonia emissions and that it is based on scientifically established methods recognised at Union or international levels.

- (14) Emission factors associated to certain animal categories and techniques, or the environmental performance of techniques may change over time due to new scientific findings. In such cases, where the change no longer allows compliance with the maximum values set out in the uniform conditions for operating rules, Member States should ensure that farmers who implemented techniques that were enabling compliance before the change occurred are not penalised. However, Member States should take the necessary measures to assist farmers to re-establish compliance.

- (15) Weaners should be defined as young pigs reared from weaning until fattening, typically reared from a live weight of around 8 kg until a live weight of around 30 kg. However, the emissions associated with weaners increase as their live weight increases. Therefore, for the purpose of calculating the installation capacity, conversion rates that reflect that increase in emissions over time have been included in the Commission’s proposal for a Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council amending Directive (EU) 2024/1785 [^ntr6-L_202602260EN.000101-E0006], by differentiating between weaners below and above 20 kg of live weight and by ensuring that weaners above 20 kg of live weight are accounted for as ‘other pigs’ for the purpose of calculating the installation capacity.

- (16) Pursuant to Article 70c(5) of Directive 2010/75/EU, the Commission is to assess the impacts of the implementation of the operating rules laid down in Article 70i of that Directive and submit, by 11 years after the entry into force of the implementing act referred to in Article 70i(2) of that Directive, a report to the European Parliament and to the Council on the results of that assessment.

- (17) In order to ensure that the uniform conditions for operating rules reflect scientific and technological developments, the Commission should ensure their periodic reviews and updates. Such updates should aim at integrating developments in best-available techniques, emerging techniques, and evolving environmental and animal welfare standards. Such updates should also take into account the findings of the Innovation centre for industrial transformation and emissions.

- (18) The Commission should update this Decision where developments in the best-available techniques allow for a significant reduction of emissions or following the assessment referred to in Article 70c(5) of Directive 2010/75/EU, where relevant.

- (19) Pursuant to Article 70i(3) of Directive 2010/75/EU, Member States are to ensure that the competent authority follows or is informed of developments in best-available techniques and of the publication of any new or updated uniform conditions for operating rules.

- (20) It is appropriate to implement targeted flexible requirements for production systems improving animal welfare recognised at Union, national or international level, which may, in certain cases, lead to higher emissions than conventional systems.

- (21) In its Communication ‘Vision for Agriculture and Food’ [^ntr7-L_202602260EN.000101-E0007], the Commission highlighted the need for a future-proof agri-food sector that functions within planetary boundaries, where farming and the food sector contribute together to the Union’s climate objectives, while preserving healthy soils, clean water and air, and protecting and restoring Union’s biodiversity. The Commission has also set the target of cutting administrative burden and meaningful simplification in policy areas that affect farmers.

- (22) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Committee referred to in Article 75 of Directive 2010/75/EU,

HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION:

#### Article 1

The uniform conditions for operating rules as referred to in Article 70i of Directive 2010/75/EU are those set out in the Annex to this Decision.

#### Article 2

1. Other techniques than those listed in the Annex to this Decision may be used. In such cases, Member States shall validate, based on scientifically established methods, that the use of such techniques, as defined in Article 3, point (10)(a), of Directive 2010/75/EU ensures at least an equivalent level of environmental protection to those listed in the Annex, including human health and climate protection, also taking into account animal welfare systems recognised at Union, national or international level.

2. Environmental performances of techniques listed in the Annex to this Decision that are different from those listed in Sections 4 and 5 of that Annex may be used. In such cases, Member States shall validate such environmental performances, based on scientifically established methods.

3. When techniques listed in the Annex to this Decision are used for which the environmental performance is not specified in Sections 4 and 5 of that Annex, Member States shall validate their environmental performance.

4. When combinations of techniques are used, Member States shall validate the environmental performance of such combinations of techniques.

5. Member States shall ensure that all underlying data required by Directive 2003/4/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr8-L_202602260EN.000101-E0008], methodologies, and assumptions used for the validations carried out in accordance with paragraphs 1 to 4 of this Article, are fully documented and publicly available.

#### Article 3

1. Member States may decide to use, as an alternative to the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air set out in the Annex to this Decision, a relative system based on minimum ammonia reduction requirements at each animal house as compared to a reference system based on Union or international sources. In this case Member States shall ensure that such system provides at least the same reduction of ammonia emissions as the one achieved when using the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air set out in that Annex, and that it is based on scientifically established methods.

2. Member States referred to in paragraph 1 shall ensure that all underlying data, required by Directive 2003/4/EC, methodologies, and assumptions are fully documented and publicly available via the internet. They shall inform the Commission about the use of the alternative approach and about the underlying data, methodologies and assumptions.

3. Member States that decide, as an alternative to the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air at each animal house, to apply maximum values of ammonia emissions to air across all animal houses at the same farm, shall ensure that there is no net increase in ammonia emissions from housing in the farm.

#### Article 4

This Decision shall apply as follows:

- (a) not later than 4 years from 8 October 2026 for installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU with a capacity of 600 LSU or more;

- (b) not later than 5 years from 8 October 2026 for installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU with a capacity of 400 LSU or more;

- (c) not later than 6 years from 8 October 2026 for all other installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU.

#### Article 5

This Decision is addressed to the Member States.

Done at Brussels, 8 October 2026.

## ANNEX

## UNIFORM CONDITIONS FOR OPERATING RULES FOR LIVESTOCK (PIGS AND POULTRY REARING)

### **SCOPE**

These Uniform Conditions (UC) concern the activities specified in Annex Ia to Directive 2010/75/EU (the IED).

The Livestock Unit (LSU) level of an installation is to be calculated using the conversion rates given in Annex Ia to the IED.

In particular, the UC cover the following on-farm processes and activities:

- (a) nutritional management;

- (b) feed preparation;

- (c) housing;

- (d) manure management, including collection, storage, processing and landspreading;

- (e) storage of dead animals.

The UC apply in parallel to other relevant legislation, e.g. Council Directive 91/676/EEC [^ntr1-L_202602260EN.000601-E0001], Directive (EU) 2016/2284 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr2-L_202602260EN.000601-E0002], Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr3-L_202602260EN.000601-E0003], legislation on animal welfare (e.g. Council Directive 2008/120/EC [^ntr4-L_202602260EN.000601-E0004], Council Directive 98/58/EC [^ntr5-L_202602260EN.000601-E0005], Council Directive 1999/74/EC [^ntr6-L_202602260EN.000601-E0006], Council Directive 2007/43/EC [^ntr7-L_202602260EN.000601-E0007]).

### **GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS**

These Uniform Conditions for operating rules are consistent with the use of best available techniques for the activities listed in Annex Ia to the IED. This document also includes information on emerging techniques, in accordance with Article 70i of the IED.

The best available techniques and emerging techniques in this document are neither prescriptive nor exhaustive. Other techniques may be used that ensure at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, including human health and climate protection.

Information on the environmental performance of the techniques listed in Section 4 and Section 5 (e.g. efficiency for ammonia emissions reduction) is based on the information exchange pursuant to Article 70i of the IED, and on recognised international guidance (e.g. UNECE guidance document on ‘Options for ammonia mitigation’). Additional information on the environmental performance of the techniques or their combinations may be taken from Union, national or international sources (e.g. UNECE guidance documents or EMEP/EEA air pollutant emission inventory guidebook) that ensure the provision of data of an equivalent scientific quality.

It is for Member States to validate, based on scientifically established methods:

- (a) the use of techniques not listed in this document that ensure at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, including human health and climate protection, also taking into account animal welfare systems recognised at Union, national or international level;

- (b) the use of environmental performance of techniques listed in this document that are different to those in Sections 4 and 5 or the environmental performance where not specified in Sections 4 and 5;

- (c) the environmental performance of combinations of techniques.

All underlying data, required by Directive 2003/4/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr8-L_202602260EN.000601-E0008], methodologies, and assumptions, must be fully documented and publicly available.

Once the ammonia is in the acidic solution (on the other side of the membrane), it combines with free protons to form non-volatile ammonium ions (NH4 <sup>+</sup>). Various inorganic or organic acids can be used to prepare the acidic solution, such as sulphuric, hydrochloric, phosphoric and lactic acid. This way, ammonia contained in the air is transformed into a valuable ammonium fertiliser (e.g. ammonium sulphate, ammonium chloride, ammonium phosphate, ammonium lactate), which can also be transported off site. This technique is suitable for both natural and forced ventilation in the buildings, as it operates independently of the ventilation system. The system is usually located outside the housing and needs sufficient space for maintenance. | Ammonia reductions of at least 75 % (in pig farms) and 45 % (in poultry farms) can be achieved in comparison to a reference system without the use of this technique. |

#### 4.4.   **On-farm processing of manure**

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Technique | Description |
| a | Mechanical separation of slurry, e.g. using: (a) screw press separator; (b) decanter-centrifuge separator; (c) separation by sieves; (d) filter pressing. | Separation of liquid and solid fractions with different dry matter content, using for example screw press separators, decanter-centrifuge separators, separation by sieves or filter pressing. Separation can be enhanced by additives or by applying different temperatures/pressures. The technique is applied, for example, when a reduction of the nitrogen and phosphorus content in the solid phase is needed due to limited available land for manure application. |
| b | Anaerobic digestion | Anaerobic microorganisms decompose the organic matter of manure in a closed reactor in the absence of oxygen. Biogas is produced and collected to serve energy generation, i.e. production of heat, combined heat and power, and/or transport fuel. Some of the heat produced is recycled in the process. The stabilised residue (digestate) can be used as fertiliser (with sufficiently solid digestate after composting). Solid manure can be co-digested with slurry and/or other co-substrates, while ensuring a dry matter content lower than 18 %. The inoculation of selected microorganisms mixtures may enhance the speed and efficiency of the process. |
| c | Manure drying with an external tunnel | Manure is collected from the laying hen houses and removed by belts that convey it outdoors to a dedicated closed structure, containing a series of perforated overlapping belts that form the tunnel. Warm air is blown through the belts, drying the manure in about two or three days. The tunnel is ventilated with air extracted from the laying hens' house. |
| d | Mechanical separation of slurry into solid and liquid fractions, followed by aerobic digestion (aeration) of the liquid fraction, with nutrient recovery | The slurry is separated into liquid and solid fractions using mechanical separation equipment and nutrients are recovered when the solid and liquid fractions are used for crop nutrition. Most of the phosphorus and a substantial portion of the nitrogen are retained in the solid fraction, which is a product with lower volume and weight. The liquid fraction is aerated, intermittently and at low intensity, by means of submerged or floating aerators in a continuous or batch process which induces biological oxidation of volatile organic compounds. The process is managed through the control of operating parameters, such as aeration intensity (dissolved oxygen concentration), pH increase limitation, temperature (e.g. between 45 <sup>o</sup>C and 75 <sup>o</sup>C) and minimising slurry mixing, with the objective of decreasing ammonia (NH3) losses to the atmosphere, while suppressing anaerobic conditions and methane formation. Ammonia released during aeration can be optionally collected (see for example the technique “Gas-permeable membranes (GPM) for ammonia capture from gaseous media”). Foam and sludge, which contain some nutrients (e.g. P) captured from the liquid fraction, are collected from the reactor and dried using mechanical separators, and then managed with the rest of the solid fraction. The technique is typically applied where pathogen and odour reduction is important prior to landspreading. Care should be taken to address possible cross-media effects. |
| e | Mechanical separation of slurry into solid and liquid fractions, followed by nitrification-denitrification of the liquid fraction, with nutrient recovery | The slurry is separated into liquid and solid fractions using mechanical separation equipment and nutrients are recovered when the solid and liquid fraction are used for crop nutrition. Most of the phosphorus and a substantial portion of the nitrogen are retained in the solid fraction, which is a product with lower volume and weight. The liquid fraction is subjected to nitrification via biological treatment under controlled aerobic conditions, in which ammonia (NH3) is oxidised to nitrites (NO2 <sup>–</sup>) and nitrates (NO3 <sup>–</sup>), thus reducing ammonia losses to the atmosphere. Coagulants and flocculants are added to enhance the process. The effluent, rich in nitrates, and also containing any remaining P, as well as K, Na and Ca, may be reused in agriculture (e.g. through fertigation). Alternatively, it can be forwarded to the denitrification stage, in which nitrite and nitrate are transformed into N2 in the presence of organic carbon. Some of the microorganisms can be recirculated to the aeration basin to maintain biological activity. Foam and sludge, which contain some nutrients (e.g. P) captured from the liquid fraction, are collected from the basin(s) and dried using mechanical separators, and then managed with the rest of the solid fraction. The nitrification-denitrification process can be achieved in a single basin or multiple basins in series. Through the setting of the retention time in the basin(s), the degree of nitrification-denitrification, including the concentration of the nitrates in the effluent, can be controlled. Care should be taken to address possible cross-media effects. |
| f | Composting of solid manure | Solid manure, including the solid fraction of the slurry, is decomposed by microorganisms under aerobic conditions, producing a final product (compost) sufficiently stable for transport, storage and landspreading. Biological inoculum, straw, sawdust, green residues or other organic wastes (e.g. digestate) can be co-composted with solid manure. Odour, microbial pathogens and water content of manure are reduced. The supply of oxygen is achieved by mechanical reversal of the windrows or by forced aeration of the heaps. Drums and composting (vertical and horizontal) tanks/silos can also be used. The inoculation of selected microorganism mixtures may enhance the speed and efficiency of the process. The technique can be used, for example, when pathogen and odour reduction is important prior to landspreading. Windrows need sufficient space to be established on the farm. Other composting options (e.g. tanks/silos, drums) usually require less space. |
| g | Vacuum evaporation with ammonia recovery | Evaporation is used to reduce digestate volume (by up to 70 %), resulting in concentrated nutrients. The liquid phase of the manure is typically evaporated under vacuum conditions at low temperatures (e.g. approximately 60 °C). During the evaporation process, gaseous ammonia is bound as an ammonium sulphate solution. Vacuum evaporation is primarily a method for reducing the volume of farm manure. |
| h | Vacuum fine separators with screens | The technique consists of a continuous fine separation technology designed for nutrient-optimised processing of liquid nutrient-rich suspensions (e.g. slurries, digestates). It enables solid-liquid separation into an energy-rich solid fraction and a nutrient-reduced liquid phase, suitable for further membrane filtration or fertiliser processing. This technique is suitable for liquids with low dry matter content (e.g. 1 %) using, for instance, fibre-retaining screens with different aperture sizes. It can be used in combination with pre-separation techniques. |
| i | Pressure and flocculation solid-liquid separators | A slurry treatment technique consisting of several stages. First, manure is divided into a solid and a liquid fraction. The liquid fraction is then subjected to a physico-chemical treatment complemented by decantation, which results in clarification and removal of suspended solids. Afterwards, the treated liquid is acidified to reduce ammonia emissions. In parallel, the solid fraction and sludge generated throughout the different stages of the liquid treatment undergo a dewatering process and are dried using solar energy. An ammonia reduction of at least 63 % can be achieved in comparison to a reference system without the use of this technique. |
| j | Mechanical separation of slurry in a solid and liquid fraction, followed by treatment in constructed wetlands of the liquid fraction, with nutrient recovery. | The slurry is separated into liquid and solid fractions using mechanical separation equipment. If there is enough space, the separation may be further enhanced by a subsequent decantation step. Most of the phosphorus and a substantial portion of the nitrogen are retained in the solid fraction, which is a product with lower volume and weight. The liquid fraction flows to the constructed wetland, where it percolates the subsurface through the gravel bed planted with specialised plant species (e.g. *Phragmites australis*, *Juncus*, *Typha*, *Apium nodiflorum*), without flooding the sand surface. Nitrogen (as NO3 <sup>–</sup> and NH4 <sup>+</sup>) is absorbed by the plants, thus reducing ammonia (NH3) losses to the atmosphere. Possible emissions of nitrous oxide (N2O) are kept low with proper management of the operational parameters. Suspended solids and Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) are also reduced. The treated effluent exits the constructed wetland by gravity into a collection pond through a drainage pipe and can be reused in agriculture (e.g. through fertigation), since it contains any remaining N and/or P. No energy consumption is needed for the operation of the constructed wetlands. The system is adequately sized (hydraulic retention time is 3-5 days) to handle the whole volume of the liquid fraction and is designed to prevent any leakage to the environment (i.e. soil and groundwater), by incorporating double-layer plastic impermeabilisation. Periodic harvesting of vegetation/biomass, which can be used as co-substrate for anaerobic digestion or as feedstock for composting, and removal of the accumulated organic matter are done as part of proper management. Care should be taken to address possible cross-media effects. |

|  | 4.8.2. | **Monitoring of ammonia emissions to air** Technique Description a Mass balance based on the excretion and the total (or total ammoniacal) nitrogen present at each manure management stage Ammonia emissions are estimated based on the amount of nitrogen excreted by each animal category and using the total nitrogen (or the total ammoniacal nitrogen – TAN) flow and the volatilisation coefficients (VC) over each manure management stage (housing, storage, landspreading). The mass balance considers in particular any significant change to the type of livestock reared at the farm and/or to the techniques applied for housing, storage and landspreading. Additionally, there are international guidance documents (e.g. the EMEP/EEA emission inventory guidebook) provididing a tiered approach for the calculation of ammonia emissions at national level. This approach consists of three possible methods (Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3) with increasing levels of complexity and accuracy. The Tier selection depends on the availability of data, significance of the livestock sector in the region, and the level of detail required. Tier 1 - This approach is a simple method that uses a single default emission factor (EF) to estimate ammonia emissions from particular animal categories. The EF is multiplied by the average annual population of each animal category to calculate emissions. The default Tier 1 EFs are calculated using Tier 2 default NH3-N EFs, default N excretion data, and default data on proportions of total ammoniacal nitrogen (TAN) in excreta. Tier 2 - This approach uses mass flow to estimate ammonia emissions from livestock. It is based on the concept of a flow of total ammoniacal nitrogen (TAN) through the manure management system, which is composed of animal housing systems or holding areas, manure storage, and field application of manure. This approach requires more input data than Tier 1, including country-specific activity data, N excretion rates, and proportions of TAN in manure. Tier 2 estimates the amount of TAN available at each stage of manure management and calculates emissions using a mass balance approach. This approach is more accurate than Tier 1 and can account for differences in management systems and resulting emissions. It also allows for the inclusion of abatement measures and can be used to estimate emissions from multiple livestock categories. Tier 3 - This approach is an advanced method that uses emission modelling and installation data to estimate ammonia emissions from livestock. It is based on the use of country-specific emission factors (EFs) or the inclusion of abatement measures. Tier 3 can utilise the calculation procedure outlined under Tier 2, but with the use of country-specific EFs or the inclusion of abatement measures. This approach requires a high level of detail and accuracy, including data on facility-specific emissions, abatement measures, and management practices. It must be well documented and accompanied by supporting literature to ensure transparency and accuracy and can provide highly accurate estimates of ammonia emissions from livestock. For the purposes of ammonia emissions monitoring, a monitoring technique based on Tier 3 is to be developed at Member State level. In order to apply the monitoring technique, farms need to keep record of information on key parameters (according to the methodology defined by the Member State), e.g. numbers of animals, feed intake, crude protein content in the diet, quantity of manure generated, on-farm manure management practices. |

#### 5.2.   **Manure storage**

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Technique | Description |
| a | Flaring of methane from slurry storage | Methane-rich gases collected from covered slurry stores are diverted to a low-pressure flare that oxidises methane to carbon dioxide and water vapour, thereby significantly reducing the climate impact compared to direct atmospheric release of methane. The technique requires slurry tanks to be equipped with systems such as dense tent covers, in order to avoid air in-leaks and maintain the methane concentration above 5 %. The gases are extracted using a vacuum pump, then pass through a condensate trap (the captured condensates are pumped back to the slurry storage) before reaching the burner, while the combustion air is added just below the burner head. Instruments such as spark igniters, as well as flame, temperature and excess oxygen sensors, are used to adjust the process to optimal combustion conditions. The energy in the combustion process can be partially recovered using heat exchangers, if a demand exists on farm. This technique does not require the use of additional fuel for pilot flame. |

#### 5.3.   **On-farm processing of manure**

- 5.4. **Monitoring**

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Technique | Description |
| a | Phosphorus recovery from manure | Phosphate salts are precipitated through the addition of ions (e.g. magnesium and potassium) and a pH regulator, often in a reactor. To optimise the process and minimise unwanted co-precipitation, it is necessary to monitor the solution’s pH and the molar ratios of the ions present in thesolution during the process. Phosphorus can be recovered in the form of salts (e.g. struvite) which can be reused as fertilisers. |
| b | Resource recovery from digestates by using gypsum | Digestate produced on farm is treated to obtain mineral fertilisers by using gypsum, which can be “natural” or “synthetic”, i.e. generated in flue-gas desulphurisation systems (gas cleaning systems used for capturing sulphur dioxide emissions from flue-gases). When gypsum is added to digestate, ammonia and carbon dioxide are gradually stripped at low temperatures/pressures (e.g. 50 to 85°C / 200 to 700 mbar, without air or steam). Precise adjustment of the chemical and thermodynamic equilibria in each step ensures low power and heat consumption and a highly efficient process. In order to decrease the power consumption, waste heat from another process can be used. The formed suspension is separated using a filter press into two mineral fertilisers: ammonium sulphate (solution) and calcium carbonate (solid). |
| c | Gas-permeable membranes (GPM) for ammonia capture from liquid media | This technique operates in two steps: in the first step, gaseous ammonia is released from ammonia-rich liquid streams (e.g. anaerobic digestate, slurry) by aeration and spraying, generating an ammonia-rich air stream; in the second step, this gas stream comes into contact with a microporous, hydrophobic membrane, where ammonia passes through the pores of the membrane. The driving force for ammonia transfer across membranes is the concentration gradient. The process is carried out at atmospheric pressure. Once the ammonia is in the acidic solution (on the other side of the membrane), it combines with free protons to form non-volatile ammonium ions (NH4 <sup>+</sup>). Various inorganic or organic acids can be used to prepare the acidic solution, such as sulphuric, hydrochloric, phosphoric and lactic acid. In this way, ammonia contained in the liquid is transformed into a valuable ammonium fertiliser (e.g. ammonium sulphate, ammonium chloride, ammonium phosphate, ammonium lactate). The system is usually located outside the housing and needs sufficient space for maintenance. An ammonia reduction of at least 30 % is expected to be achieved in comparison to a reference system without the use of this technique. |
