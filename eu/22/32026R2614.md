THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) 2015/478 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 March 2015 on common rules for imports [^ntr1-L_202602614EN.000101-E0001], and in particular Articles 5 and 7 thereof,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) 2015/755 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2015 on common rules for imports from certain third countries [^ntr2-L_202602614EN.000101-E0002], and in particular Articles 3 and 4 thereof,

Having regard to the Agreement on the European Economic Area [^ntr3-L_202602614EN.000101-E0003], and in particular Article 113(3), second paragraph, thereof

Whereas:

### 1. **BACKGROUND**

- (1) On 30 October 2026, the European Commission (‘the Commission’) initiated on its own initiative a safeguard investigation with regard to imports into the Union of new hybrid vehicles designed for the transport of persons (‘hybrids’) pursuant to Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2015/478 and Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2015/755 (‘the basic regulations’). It published a Notice of Initiation in the *Official Journal of the European Union* [^ntr4-L_202602614EN.000101-E0004] (‘the Notice of Initiation’).

- (2) The initiation followed an in-depth analysis of the information available to the Commission from public market intelligence sources and the Union industry. In view of the sensitivity of the hybrids sector, its strategic importance to the Union economy in terms of innovation, electrification and employment, and the existence of sufficient evidence showing that imports of hybrids threaten to cause serious injury to the Union industry, the Commission decided, in these special circumstances and in accordance with the basic regulations, to initiate the investigation without having received a written request from a Member State.

- (3) The information available to the Commission further indicated that imports were entering the Union at an accelerating pace, and at prices significantly below those of the Union industry. In light of the fragile situation of the Union industry, the existence of significant global production capacities and the attractiveness of the Union market, this surge in imports has prompted the Commission to assess whether critical circumstances exist requiring the introduction without delay of provisional measures in order to prevent serious injury that would be difficult to remedy.

- (4) The need to act without delay required the Commission to carry out its analysis on the basis of the information available at this stage. This does not prejudge the consideration of further information to be obtained during the investigation. In particular, questionnaire replies from Union producers, importers and dealers, written submissions and any information submitted in the exercise of the rights of defence will be duly examined by the Commission during the investigation. Interested parties will also have the opportunity to request hearings, in accordance with Section 3.4 of the Notice of Initiation.

### 2. **PRODUCT CONCERNED AND LIKE OR DIRECTLY COMPETING PRODUCTS**

- (5) The product concerned as defined in the Notice of Initiation is certain new hybrid vehicles, principally designed for the transport of nine or less persons, including the driver, with both internal combustion piston engine and one or more electric motor[s], as motors for propulsion, regardless of the number of wheels set in motion, whether capable or not of being charged by plugging to an external source of electric power, currently falling under CN codes ex 8703 40 10 , ex 8703 50 00 , ex 8703 60 10 , and ex 8703 70 00 (TARIC code 8703 40 10 10, 8703 40 10 90, 8703 50 00 10, 8703 60 10 10, 8703 60 10 90, 8703 70 00 10).

- (6) The product concerned consists of two types: new hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) and new plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs). PHEVs have an internal combustion piston engine and one or more battery-powered electric motors. The combustion engine supports the electric motor, and the battery is recharged externally, by connecting to the grid. HEVs are powered by an internal combustion piston engine but also have a battery-powered electric motor that serves to complement the conventional engine. Their electricity is generated internally from regenerative braking and the internal combustion engine [^ntr5-L_202602614EN.000101-E0005]. HEVs include so called mild hybrid electric vehicles (MHEVs), which are vehicles equipped with a battery that assists the internal combustion engine in propelling the vehicle to improve fuel economy.

- (7) Vehicles with an electric power source that is used only for non-propulsion functions, L-category vehicles according to Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr6-L_202602614EN.000101-E0006], and motorcycles are excluded from the investigation.

- (8) The Commission provisionally determined that the product produced by the Union producers, referred to as the ‘like product’, is like or directly competing with the product concerned. Both the domestic and the imported product possess the same fundamental physical and technical characteristics. Both incorporate an internal combustion engine combined with an electric motor and battery system. While individual models may differ in terms of performance, equipment or design, such differences do not alter their fundamental nature as HEVs or PHEVs. Both the domestic and imported product serve identical purposes, they are used for the transport of persons and thus have the same end-use. In addition, both are offered through similar or identical sales channels to customers who may acquire them from both domestic and foreign suppliers. Accordingly, there exists a significant level of competition between the product concerned and the one produced by the Union producers.

### 3. **THE UNION PRODUCERS**

- (9) Based on market intelligence, the Union producers of the like product include manufacturers operating under various brands. ACEA (the European Automobile Manufacturers’ Association) represents a number of these Union producers.

- (10) In the Notice of Initiation, Union producers have been requested to cooperate with the Commission in the investigation.

### 4. **INCREASE IN IMPORTS**

- (11) Based on the information from Eurostat, the Commission has carried out a preliminary analysis of the evolution of imports of the product concerned over the period 2023-2025 and the first half of 2026. In order to ensure comparability with previous full-year periods, the Commission also analysed the most recent 12-month period comprising the last six months of 2025 and the first six months of 2026 (the ‘MRP’ or ‘most recent period’).

- (12) In its assessment of import evolution, the Commission did not take into account the import volumes from certain countries that should be excluded from the scope of the provisional measures, in particular: the European Economic Area (EEA) countries, Kenya, and Ukraine. On account of the close integration of markets with EEA members and the low import volumes from these countries, the Commission considers that the products under assessment originating in Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein should be excluded from the application of this Regulation. Imports from Kenya are excluded based on the bilateral agreement [^ntr7-L_202602614EN.000101-E0007] [^ntr8-L_202602614EN.000101-E0008], and Ukraine is excluded based on the suspension of the basic safeguard regulation [^ntr9-L_202602614EN.000101-E0009]. No imports from Kenya were reported during the period considered, and the imports from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Ukraine represented together less than 0,01 % of the total EU imports of the product concerned.

### 5. **UNFORESEEN DEVELOPMENTS**

- (20) The Commission preliminarily concluded that the increase in imports is the result of unforeseen developments that find its source in a number of factors causing imbalances in the international trade of the product concerned.

- (21) First, the global automotive industry has been characterised by rapidly growing electric-vehicle production, including the product concerned, and manufacturing capacity, particularly in China, where this rapid capacity expansion has been supported by industrial policies, five-year plans, and other government support programmes [^ntr10-L_202602614EN.000101-E0010]. This could not reasonably have been anticipated at the time of the relevant WTO tariff negotiations or at the time that the EU agreed to China's accession to the WTO [^ntr11-L_202602614EN.000101-E0011]. A Mobility Global forecast of general plant capacity in China, Türkiye, Korea, Japan and the UK estimates that the upward trend in capacity will continue between 2026 and 2033, driven primarily by the capacity expansions in China (Table 16).

- (22) At the same time, major export markets are closing as a result of increasing trade-restrictive practices affecting electric vehicles, and also the product concerned. In particular, the United States applies significant import barriers, including Section 301 duties of 100 % on PHEVs manufactured in China in addition to MFN duties of 2,5 %, as well as local content-related requirements, and 25 % Section 232 duties on imports from all other origins. Other major markets, including Canada and Türkiye, also maintain substantial tariff barriers. Canada maintains a quota on electric passenger cars from China, including certain hybrid vehicles, of 49 000 vehicles per year at a most-favoured-nation tariff rate of 6,1 %. Türkiye currently maintains additional customs duty of 40 % on passenger cars originating in China, including certain PHEVs. By contrast, the Union market remains comparatively open (10 % MFN). These trade-restrictive measures, that make the Union an attractive export destination, could not reasonably have been anticipated at the time of the relevant WTO tariff negotiations.

- (23) Following the anti-subsidy investigation into battery electric vehicles (BEVs) from China, the Union introduced additional duties in the form of countervailing measures. Provisional measures entered into force on 4 July 2024, and definitive measures on 30 October 2024. The countervailing duties range from 17 % for BYD to 35,3 % for SAIC and other non-cooperating producers. As a result, the Union market for Chinese electric vehicle exports became more restricted. Prior to the imposition of measures, imports of BEVs into the Union followed a clear and significant upward trend, increasing year-on-year (see Table 6).

### 6. **OBLIGATIONS WHOSE EFFECT RESULTED IN THE INCREASE IN IMPORTS**

- (27) The Commission preliminarily concluded that the increase in imports of the product concerned is the result of obligations undertaken by the European Union under the GATT 1994, in particular the tariff concessions granted on the relevant tariff lines in past rounds of multilateral trade negotiations.

- (28) The product concerned comprises several tariff lines and on all of these tariff lines, the European Union has the tariff commitments of 10 % ad valorem.

- (29) These concessions are currently inscribed in Part I, Section II of the European Union’s Schedule of Concessions and Commitments, certified as Schedule EU CLXXIII – European Union on 1 December 2016 [^ntr13-L_202602614EN.000101-E0013], as amended.

- (30) As a result of these concessions inscribed in the European Union’s Schedule of Concessions and Commitments, annexed to the GATT and incorporated pursuant to Article II:7 of the GATT 1994 and as a result of other parts of the GATT 1994, the European Union has obligations incurred under the GATT 1994 notably as follows: Article XI:1, prohibiting non-tariff restrictions on the importation of the product concerned, and, importantly, Article II:1(a) and Article II:1(b), first and second sentences, of the GATT 1994. Under Article II:1(b), first sentence, the EU is not allowed, absent an applicable exception, to impose ordinary customs duties on the product concerned in excess of those set forth and provided in the relevant part of the EU’s Schedule of Concessions and Commitments. Furthermore, the EU’s applied import tariff on the product concerned, also known as the most-favoured-nation tariffs, at the time of the investigation, was at the maximum levels allowed under the tariff commitments indicated above.

- (31) The product concerned was being imported in increased quantities as a result of the above obligations undertaken under the GATT 1994 [^ntr14-L_202602614EN.000101-E0014], because those obligations, combined with the European Union’s tariff concessions, enhanced and secured the conditions of market access for imports of new hybrid vehicles to the Union market. The above-mentioned tariff commitments also give no leeway for the European Union to increase ordinary customs duties. This simultaneously explains how the obligations in question resulted in the increase in imports that threaten to cause serious injury.

- (32) The GATT obligations specified simultaneously prevented the EU from increasing the applied import duties on the product at issue. These were, thus, the European Union’s ‘obligations of the GATT 1994 [which] constrain[ed] its ability to prevent or remedy injury from an increase in imports’. Concomitantly, the European Union ‘suspend[ed] those obligations as a result’ of its safeguard measure

#### 7.2.   **Threat of serious injury**

- (52) The Commission provisionally concluded that the situation of the Union industry does currently not yet point to the existence of serious injury caused by the increasing imports. Nevertheless, the Commission considered that the industry is in a fragile situation and that there is a threat of serious injury, in view of the declining economic indicators presented in Section 7.1, the significant and accelerating increase in low-priced imports, the planned restructurings within the Union industry, and the significant available global production capacity.

*Declining economic indicators*

- (53) The previous analysis showed that, in the most recent period, the Union producers came under increasing pressure in terms of market share, capacity utilisation, profitability and prices. Despite a significant increase in the Union consumption, Union producers saw their market share decline by 2 percentage points [^ntr19-L_202602614EN.000101-E0019] between 2025 and the MRP, and the capacity utilisation remained persistently low, between 61 % and 63 % throughout the period considered. The low utilisation rates of the plants translated in higher fixed costs for the Union producers in a situation where the Union producers reasonably expected to increase the production outputs and to decrease the fixed cost of production. The loss of market share is particularly concerning at a time when Union manufacturers are required to make substantial investments in the development of new models and technologies as part of the transition towards electrification.

- (54) At the same time, the imports surged, with import prices averaging 18 % below those of the Union industry and the Union industry’s profitability deteriorated continuously over the period considered. This cumulative evidence underscored that the Union industry was unable to benefit from the market growth, as low-priced imports aggressively captured incremental demand to the direct detriment of the domestic producers' economic performance.

*Planned restructurings*

- (63) Based on a preliminary analysis of the European Restructuring Monitor (ERM) [^ntr22-L_202602614EN.000101-E0022] and complementary company and media reporting, there appeared to have been an intensification of restructuring activity among automotive producers in the Union since 2024. The analysis below focuses on the major Union automotive producers.

- (64) In December 2024, a Union producer committed to reducing its workforce by more than 35 000 employees across its German locations by 2030 [^ntr23-L_202602614EN.000101-E0023]. This figure was subsequently revised upwards in March 2026, with the company expecting around 50 000 jobs to be eliminated across the Group in the EU by 2030 [^ntr24-L_202602614EN.000101-E0024].

- (65) In September 2026, the Union producer approved plans to cut a further 50 000 jobs globally. This brings the total number of positions the producer plans to eliminate by 2030 to approximately 100 000, comprising around 50 000 jobs confirmed across the Union and an additional 50 000 positions worldwide, roughly half of the latter in Germany [^ntr25-L_202602614EN.000101-E0025]. Although these job cuts are broad in scope and do not specifically target HEV or PHEV production, the future production of hybrid vehicles is nevertheless under increasing pressure as a result of these large-scale restructuring announcements as to ensure capacity reduction. For example, the September 2026 announcement indicated that the Union producer could not currently guarantee future production allocations for the Hannover (12 900 employees), Emden (7 700 employees), Zwickau (8 000 employees) and Audi Neckarsulm (15 500 employees) plants after 2030, in the face of surging import competition [^ntr26-L_202602614EN.000101-E0026]. Both the Hannover and Neckarsulm plants are involved in the production of new hybrid vehicles, highlighting the threat to employment linked to new hybrid vehicle production at these sites. In addition, several production lines in the Union are also sufficiently flexible to manufacture vehicles with different powertrains, meaning that closure of any such plant will have consequences for future potential production of HEVs and PHEVs.

- (66) The Commission observed that another Union producer of hybrid vehicles aims to improve capacity utilisation to 80 % by 2030 by restructuring and reducing European production capacity by 800 000 units [^ntr27-L_202602614EN.000101-E0027]. As part of this restructuring, the Union producer plans to end vehicle production at Poissy (France) by the end of 2028 at the earliest. This will include the production of hybrid vehicles. The site is instead expected to be repurposed for activities including parts production, vehicle reconditioning, recycling, 3D printing and other industrial activities.

- (67) This indicates that while demand for electrified vehicles is increasing, the market growth is predominantly captured by imported hybrids and the Union producers are planning to reduce production capacity rather than being able to fully benefit from this market growth. The expected continuation of the surge in low-priced imports will further aggravate the pressure on Union producers.

- (68) The Commission observed additional large-scale restructuring announcements, which are directly linked to the increased global pressure and aggressive pricing in the automotive sector, and will likely impact the future production of hybrid vehicles. For instance, another automotive producer announced a comprehensive new long-term strategy and restructuring addressing intense global competition, development costs and rising pressures from fast-moving Chinese manufacturers [^ntr28-L_202602614EN.000101-E0028]. This Union producer plans to cut between 15 % to 20 % of its global engineering workforce [^ntr29-L_202602614EN.000101-E0029] and targets the reduction in technical, research, and administrative engineering positions globally, however impacting significantly operations in France and Spain, to lower electrified vehicle development costs.

- (69) Additionally, the Commission noted announcements from another Union hybrid vehicle producer regarding a global workforce reduction of nearly 8 000 employees through 2027 [^ntr30-L_202602614EN.000101-E0030], with the majority of cuts concentrated in Germany. The company pointed to eroding profitability and severe market headwinds as the core catalysts for the reorganisation.

- (70) Furthermore, another Union producer has undertaken structural downsizing and cost-containment strategies to preserve profitability. Amid declining high-end vehicle demand and intense pricing pressures in China, the resulting strain on global revenues has necessitated scaled-back operations, reduced production targets, and a comprehensive re-evaluation of the company's cost architecture [^ntr31-L_202602614EN.000101-E0031].

#### 7.3.   **Conclusion**

- (76) Considering the above, the Commission preliminarily concluded that the Union producers were in a vulnerable situation and faced a threat of serious injury, as evidenced by the declining economic indicators presented in Section 7.1, the significant and accelerating increase in low-priced imports, the planned restructurings within the Union industry, the significantly available global production capacities and the increasingly present stocks in the Union market.

### 8. **CAUSATION**

- (77) The Commission examined whether the increased imports caused the threat of serious injury to the Union producers. In addition, the Commission also examined whether other known factors could at the same time have contributed to the threat of serious injury to the Union producers as to call into question the presence of a causal link between the threat of serious injury suffered by the Union industry and the increased imports.

#### 8.1.   **Increased imports**

- (78) The Commission has made a preliminary determination that there was a causal link between the increased imports of the product concerned and the threat of serious injury suffered by the Union producers. The causal link between the surge in imports and the fragile economic situation of the Union industry was demonstrated by the developments observed in the most recent period.

- (79) Although Union consumption and Union sales increased during the period considered, the most recent period stands out as the first in which market growth was captured predominantly by imports rather than by the Union industry. As imports aggressively expanded their presence in the Union market during this period, the Union industry experienced a deterioration in its economic indicators, including a loss of market share, significant price pressure, increasingly fragile profitability and the need for further restructuring.

- (80) Monthly data for the most recent period indicate that imports are entering the Union market at an accelerating pace. Furthermore, exporters have sufficiently freely available capacity, with further expansions planned. Given the attractiveness of the Union market for hybrids in terms of price, the import trend observed in the most recent period is expected to continue and even further accelerate.

- (81) Given the already fragile profitability of the Union industry, coupled with the high fixed costs and substantial investments required to remain competitive in this market, the continuation of the accelerating import trend will undoubtedly further deteriorate the industry's economic situation (as it did in the most recent period) and is likely to cause serious injury to Union producers in the very near future.

- (82) The Commission therefore preliminarily concluded that there was a causal link between the increase in imports and the threat of serious injury suffered by Union producers.

#### 8.2.   **Other known factors**

- (83) To ensure that the threat of serious injury is not attributed to factors other than increased imports, the Commission has carried out a preliminary analysis to determine whether other factors may have contributed to the threat of serious injury suffered by the Union producers and to distinguish the effects of those factors from the effects of increased imports, with a view to preliminarily establishing the existence of a genuine and substantial relationship of cause and effect.

- (84) While high energy prices are an important factor in the production of hybrid vehicles, the actual threat of serious injury stems from the pressure exercised on the EU prices by the surge of low-priced imports. Indeed, energy prices are generally higher in the Union than in some other parts of the world. Under normal market conditions, manufacturers can reflect cost increases in their sales prices in order to maintain reasonable profits. However, the product concerned enters the Union on average at prices 18 % lower than Union prices and exercises pressure which prevented the Union producers from increasing their sales price to cover for the energy price. It is thus not the increasing energy costs as such, but, due to the increased imports, the Union producers’ inability to accurately reflect these cost increases in their sales prices, that a threat of serious injury exists.

- (85) As regards the export performance of the Union producers, it has been largely stable over the period considered with the exception of 2024, in which exports decreased. Given this stability, the Commission provisionally considered that the export performance did not contribute to the threat of serious injury.

- (86) As regards consumer demand, registrations of hybrid vehicles in the EU have been growing consistently since 2023. In addition, according to Global Mobility, registrations are expected to continue increasing until 2029.

- (89) As regards the regulatory environment relating to the reduction of CO2 emissions, this cannot be considered as a cause of the threat of serious injury. On the contrary, the applicable and proposed regulatory developments have improved the outlook for hybrid vehicle producers. While the Union has set ambitious CO2 reduction targets for new passenger cars and vans, these measures do not amount to an immediate prohibition on hybrid technologies. Rather, the transition framework has been structured over time and has recently evolved in a manner that is less restrictive for hybrid producers than previously anticipated. In particular, the policy pathway has shifted from expectations of a more accelerated full battery-electric transition by 2030 towards a more gradual approach towards 2035, and the proposal of a 90 % CO2 reduction benchmark instead of a full ban of non-BEV vehicles leaves scope for the continued presence of hybrid vehicles. Accordingly, the Commission considers that the regulatory environment cannot currently be characterised as threatening to cause serious injury to Union hybrid producers.

- (90) Consequently, the Commission has not provisionally identified any other factors that would attenuate the causal link between the increase in imports and the threat of serious injury to the Union producers.

#### 8.3.   **Conclusion**

- (91) For the above reasons, the Commission provisionally concluded that there was a causal link between the significant increase of imports and the threat of serious injury suffered by the Union industry. Other factors may have contributed to the threat of serious injury suffered by Union producers, but these factors did not attenuate the causal link.

### 9. **UNION INTEREST**

- (92) The Commission examined whether it would be in the Union interest to adopt provisional measures. The analysis of the Union interest was based on an appraisal of all the various interests involved, including those of the Union producers, importers and users.

- (93) The climate neutrality objective is at the heart of the [European Green Deal](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/priorities-2019-2024/european-green-deal_en), and is a legally binding objective set out in the Climate Law. To reach its climate neutrality goal by 2050 [^ntr32-L_202602614EN.000101-E0032], the EU is taking action to reduce emissions from cars as road transport accounts for one fifth of the EU’s CO2 emissions [^ntr33-L_202602614EN.000101-E0033]. The EU aims to cut emissions from cars by 55 % and from vans by 50 % by 2030, compared with 2021, in order to reach the goal of zero emissions from new cars and vans by 2035 [^ntr34-L_202602614EN.000101-E0034]. Therefore, the sale of new petrol or diesel cars is (for the moment) banned from 2035 [^ntr35-L_202602614EN.000101-E0035].

- (94) However, during the current period of transition between combustion engine cars and fully electric cars, hybrid cars are a frequently used and sought after alternative for consumers as they already contribute to emission reduction but do not have the disadvantage of lower driving range as many fully electric cars.

- (95) The same applies to the car manufacturers, who also use hybrid cars as transitional models between the combustion engine and fully electric cars to spread costs and as a ‘lab’ for research and innovation to pave the way to state of the art fully electric cars.

- (96) Therefore, losing hybrids production in the Union would leave consumers with few alternatives, as the market share of Chinese imports is already erasing the market shares of other suppliers such as Japan or Korea, and during the MRP Chinese imports also began to gain market share at the expense of the Union industry.

- (97) Furthermore, this market is characterised by high fixed costs and substantive investments requirements necessary to remain competitive. A viable and profitable car industry in the Union is essential to ensure the necessary investments in research and development to produce fully electric cars by 2035 in the Union. It would not be in the Union interest to let the EU’s green transition be driven solely by foreign players.

- (98) In addition, the Commission considers that a provisional safeguard measure in the form outlined below in Section 12 would strike an appropriate balance between the interests of Union producers and downstream users. Taking into account the importance of the hybrid vehicle sector to the Union economy, in particular in terms of innovation, electrification and employment, the Commission's preliminary assessment was that the imposition of measures on hybrid vehicles would be in the Union interest.

### 10. **CRITICAL CIRCUMSTANCES**

- (99) In accordance with Article 7, paragraph 1, letter (a) of Regulation (EU) 2015/478, the Commission examined whether critical circumstances existed where delay would cause damage which would be difficult to repair. To this end, the Commission assessed the situation of the Union producers, the developments in imports in the most recent period and the likelihood that imports would increase further in the immediate future.

- (100) As explained in Section 7, despite significant growth in Union consumption, the Union producers lost market share during the most recent period, while their profitability declined substantially, reaching only around two-thirds of the minimum profit normally required to ensure the long-term financial viability of their hybrids business. At the same time, imports entered the Union at prices on average 18 % below those of the Union industry. The Union producers therefore faced increasing competitive pressure at a time when their capacity to absorb further deterioration in their economic situation is already limited.

- (101) Monthly data confirmed that the surge in low-priced imports continued, notably, but not only, for imports originating in China. Chinese imports have entered the Union at an accelerated pace, in particular in the second half of the MRP, and gained further market share at the expense of the Union industry as well as other traditional suppliers to the Union market. At the same time, domestic demand in China is declining year on year, while production in China is expected to continue increasing, although capacity utilisation is estimated to be as low as 43 %. This combination of excess capacity, weakening domestic demand and intense domestic competition creates a strong incentive for Chinese producers to seek out export markets in the imminent future. The increasing use of trade-restrictive measures in other markets further heightens the risk that these exports will be directed towards the Union, which remains a relatively open and attractive market. The Union is therefore exposed to the risk of an imminent further increase in imports at a time when its industry is already in a fragile situation.

- (102) The Commission considered that the combination of the already fragile economic situation of the Union producers, the continuing increase in imports, the substantial price pressure exerted by those imports and the likelihood of further import increases in the immediate future creates a situation in which any delay in the adoption of provisional safeguard measures would cause damage which would be difficult to repair. In the absence of timely action, the Union industry would face further increases in import volumes and continued price pressure before the investigation could be concluded.

- (103) The Commission therefore provisionally concluded that critical circumstances exist within the meaning of Article 7(1)(a) of Regulation (EU) 2015/478 and that provisional safeguard measures should be adopted without delay.

### 12. **CONCLUSIONS AND ADOPTION OF PROVISIONAL MEASURES**

#### 12.1.   **Adoption of provisional measures**

- (110) It was preliminarily concluded that the Union producers are suffering a threat of serious injury. Given the critical circumstances explained in Section 10, it is considered that provisional safeguard measures should be taken in order to prevent further damage to the Union industry which would be difficult to repair before the conclusion of the current investigation.

#### 12.2.   **Form, level and duration of the provisional measure**

- (111) In determining the most appropriate form of provisional safeguard measure, the Commission considered several elements.

- (112) First, the Commission considered what is appropriate to remedy the threat of serious injury to the Union producers, which became evident during the most recent period.

- (113) Second, the Commission took into account its practice in previous safeguard investigations and the applicable international obligations, according to which traditional trade flows should be maintained to the extent that they do not exacerbate the threat of serious injury established.

- (114) Third, the Commission considered the price levels at which imports enter the Union, the specific market circumstances, and the ability of exporting producers to absorb very high additional duties [^ntr39-L_202602614EN.000101-E0039]. The measure should provide effective protection to Union producers from the increased imports that cause a threat of serious injury.

- (115) In light of the above considerations, in particular with regard to the critical circumstances making immediate action necessary, the Commission considered that a tariff measure would not provide an effective response to the threat of serious injury, as it would not sufficiently prevent the increased imports from materialising. A quantitative restriction in the form of import quotas, setting a maximum quantity of the product concerned that may be imported into the Union during the period of application of the provisional safeguard measure, was therefore considered necessary to provide effective protection to Union producers. Imports outside the quotas should not be allowed. When imports are mentioned, it should be read as customs declarations for release for free circulation.

- (116) The Commission ensured that this measure is compatible with the Union’s obligations under bilateral or regional trade agreements with third countries. The Commission decided to impose a measure in the form of quotas. The Commission concluded that the adoption of such a measure constitutes the least disturbing measure for the continuation of bilateral trade with the Union’s free trade partners which is strictly necessary to remedy the difficulties that have arisen.

- (117) Based on Union interest considerations and in order to ensuring optimal use of the quota volumes, the Commission considered that quotas should be allocated on the one hand specifically to countries having a substantial interest in supplying the product concerned (so called ‘country-specific quotas’, or ‘CSQs’) and, on the other hand, to all other origins. For the purpose of this Regulation, it is considered that countries with a share of more than 2 % of imports in the year 2025 have a significant supplying interest and should receive a country-specific quota. A residual quota (‘the other countries quota’) should be allocated to all other supplying countries.

- (118) The Commission considered it appropriate to set the quota volumes of country-specific quotas at the level of imports recorded of the respective country in 2025, increased by a value slightly above 15 %. This approach took account of the significant growth of the Union market for new hybrid vehicles, which expanded by an average of 15 % over the preceding three years. The Commission considered that setting the quotas at historical trade levels alone would not adequately reflect the development of the market or the interests of Union users. The year 2025 was provisionally selected as the reference year because it was the most recent full year with available data. In addition, 2025 recorded the highest import volumes.

- (119) In view of the limited number of vehicles imported from the other origins besides the origins with a substantial interest in supplying the product concerned (‘the country-specific quota holders’), and in order to avoid unduly restricting their access to the Union market, the other countries annual quota was set at 1 500 vehicles, rather than being calculated in accordance with the methodology described in recital 118.

- (120) The quotas are set out in Annex II to this Regulation. The quotas should be administered on a quarterly basis, with unused quota volumes carried over to the following quarter (from 1 December 2026 to 28 February 2027, from 1 March 2027 to 31 May 2027, and from 1 June 2027 to 18 June 2027), within the period of application of the provisional safeguard measure.

- (121) The other countries quota should be filled based on the chronological order of the dates on which declarations of release for free circulation are accepted, as provided for in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447 [^ntr40-L_202602614EN.000101-E0040]. This method of administration calls for close cooperation between the Member States and the Commission.

- (122) Imports within the applicable quota shall be subject to the customs duty rate otherwise applicable to the product concerned, taking into account any preferential tariff treatment provided for under an agreement between the Union and the country of origin.

- (123) The eligibility of imported goods from developing countries to be excluded from the quotas is dependent on the origin of the goods. The criteria for determining non-preferential origin currently in force in the Union should therefore be applied.

- (124) Imports in excess of the applicable quota should not be permitted.

- (125) As the quotas are expressed in numbers of vehicles, only whole units can be allocated. Where a quota volume is calculated pro rata temporis, or where drawing requests exceeding the available balance are allocated on a pro rata basis, the result should therefore be rounded down to the nearest whole unit, so that the quota is never exceeded. So as not to reduce the overall quota volume, the balance resulting from rounding down should not remain available for drawing in the current quota period but should be carried over to the following one, except in the last quota period.

- (126) The provisional measures should apply for 200 calendar days (until 18 June 2027) from the date on which this Regulation enters into force. To make sure these rules are operationalised as soon as possible, they should apply from the entry into force of this Regulation, without any blocking period.

- (127) The product under investigation is set out in recital (5). However, the production processes and market conditions in the automotive sector as a whole are characterised by a high degree of complexity. In particular, the product in question may be manufactured in production facilities that also produce vehicles outside the scope of the product definition, including through the use of same components. HAS ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:

#### Article 1

1. Subject to Article 4, quotas are hereby opened in relation to imports into the Union of certain new hybrid vehicles, originating in third countries, principally designed for the transport of nine or less persons, including the driver, with both internal combustion piston engine and one or more electric motor[s] as motors for propulsion, regardless of the number of wheels set in motion, whether capable or not of being charged by plugging to an external source of electric power, currently falling under CN codes ex 8703 40 10 , ex 8703 50 00 , ex 8703 60 10 , and ex 8703 70 00 (TARIC codes 8703 40 10 10, 8703 40 10 90, 8703 50 00 10, 8703 60 10 10, 8703 60 10 90, 8703 70 00 10) for a period of 200 calendar days from the entry into force of this Regulation.

2. Where a quota is exhausted, the imports of the product concerned shall not be allowed to enter the Union.

3. The declaration of the quotas for the origin concerned shall be mandatory.

4. Imports within the applicable quota shall be subject to the customs duty rate otherwise applicable to the product concerned, taking into account any preferential tariff treatment provided for under an agreement between the Union and the country of origin.

5. Notwithstanding the first paragraph, imports of the product concerned brought into Northern Ireland from another part of the United Kingdom and sold to final consumers in the territory of Northern Ireland and registered for use in Northern Ireland shall not be subject to quotas.

6. This Article shall not apply to products originating in:

- (a) Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway;

- (b) Ukraine;

- (c) Kenya;

- (d) developing countries as specified in Annex I.1 with the exception of the countries specified in Annex I.2.

#### Article 2

1. The quotas set out in Article 1 shall be managed by the Commission and the Member States in accordance with the management system for tariff quotas provided for in Articles 49 to 54 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447.

2. The quotas shall be administered quarterly: from 1 December 2026 to 28 February 2027, from 1 March 2027 to 31 May 2027, and from 1 June 2027 to 18 June 2027.

3. The allocation of each quarterly quota shall be stopped on the 20th working day of the Commission following the end of the quarterly period.

4. From 1 December 2026 to 18 June 2027, the unused volumes of quotas in one quarter shall be carried over to the next quarter within the same yearly period of application.

5. Quantities attributed under a quota may be returned in accordance with the applicable customs legislation. Where drawings on a quarterly quota have been stopped in accordance with Article 2(3) of this Regulation, or where a quota has been exhausted or otherwise closed to further access pursuant to this Regulation, the return of such quantities shall not entail the reopening of that quota for the submission or acceptance of new drawing requests. Such returned quantities shall be taken into account only for the purpose of adjusting the balance of the relevant quota. Where the unused balance of a quarterly quota has already been transferred to the following quarter in accordance with Article 2(3) of this Regulation, quantities returned thereafter in respect of that quarter shall not be transferred to the following quarter. Therefore, those quotas will no longer be accessible to economic operators.

6. By way of derogation from Article 51(5) of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447, quotas distributed by this Regulation shall not be subject to a blocking period.

7. Where the quantities requested exceed the available balance of a quota, the quantities to be allocated shall be determined on a pro rata basis and, where the result is not a whole number, rounded down to the nearest whole unit. The quota shall be closed as from the date of that allocation. The balance remaining as a result of rounding down shall not be available for further drawings within that quota period and shall be carried over to the following quota period, unless the quota period concerned is the last quota period within the same yearly period of application.

#### Article 3

1. The imports of the products listed in the Annex III to this Regulation shall be subject to retrospective Union surveillance in accordance with Regulations (EU) 2015/478 and (EU) 2015/755.

2. The classification of the products covered by this Regulation is based on CN and TARIC.

#### Article 4

1. The origin of any product to which this Regulation applies shall be determined in accordance with the provisions in force in the Union relating to non-preferential origin and with Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council [^ntr41-L_202602614EN.000101-E0041].

2. Unless otherwise specified, the relevant provisions in force concerning customs duties shall apply.

#### Article 5

1. The benefit of the quotas referred to in Article 1 for goods originating in the People’s Republic of China shall be subject to presentation of a signed and stamped export quota certificate issued by the China Chamber of Commerce for Import and Export of Machinery and Electronic Products (CCCME) of the People’s Republic of China.

2. The export quota certificate shall be made out on a form corresponding to the model in Annex IV to this Regulation.

#### Article 6

The Member States and the Commission shall cooperate closely to ensure compliance with this Regulation.

#### Article 7

This Regulation shall enter into force on 1 December 2026.

This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.

Done at Brussels, 23 November 2026.
