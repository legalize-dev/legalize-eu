THE COUNCIL OF THE EUROPEAN UNION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) 2024/792 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 February 2024 establishing the Ukraine Facility [^ntr1-L_202602596EN.000101-E0001], and in particular Article 26(4) thereof,

Having regard to the proposal from the European Commission,

Whereas:

- (1) Pillar I of the Ukraine Facility (the ‘Facility’), established by Regulation (EU) 2024/792, makes financial support of up to EUR 46 873 049 093,86, as set out in Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2024/1447 [^ntr2-L_202602596EN.000101-E0002], and the equivalent in EUR of NOK 1 000 000 000, as set out in that Decision [^ntr3-L_202602596EN.000101-E0003], available to Ukraine for the period 2024-2027.

- (2) The financial support takes the form of non-repayable support and loans.

- (3) Financial support under Pillar I of the Facility is allocated largely on the basis of the Ukraine Plan (the ‘Plan’) [^ntr4-L_202602596EN.000101-E0004], which the Council approved by Implementing Decision (EU) 2024/1447. The Plan sets out the steps that Ukraine must fulfil in order to receive the quarterly instalments.

- (4) To date, EUR 32 455 384 486,86 has been disbursed to Ukraine under Pillar I of the Facility. Of that amount, EUR 4 431 301 973,86 has been disbursed in the form of non-repayable financial support and EUR 20 134 082 513 in the form of loans under the first eight instalments of the Plan. In addition, Ukraine received EUR 6 000 000 000 as exceptional bridge financing and EUR 1 890 000 000 as pre-financing, pursuant to Articles 24 and 25 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792.

- (5) In accordance with the loan agreement concluded between the Union and Ukraine pursuant to Article 22 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792, an amount of EUR 1 455 253 522 from those first eight instalments was used to clear the pre-financing of the Facility loan.

- (6) On 30 September 2026, Ukraine submitted a request for the payment of part of the fourth, seventh, eighth and ninth instalments under the Plan, amounting to EUR 8 305 265 116. The request also covered one step in the tenth instalment.

- (7) In particular, the request was accompanied by documents presented as evidence that Ukraine had fulfilled one step in the fourth instalment, three steps in the seventh instalment, one step in the eight instalment, seventeen steps in the ninth instalment and one step in the tenth instalment. Ukraine also submitted documents required under the agreements concluded pursuant to Articles 9, 10 and 22 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792 [^ntr5-L_202602596EN.000101-E0005].

- (8) The steps underpinning Ukraine’s latest payment request relate to various reforms and investments set out in the Plan which broadly fall under the chapters on the judicial system; the fight against corruption and money laundering; financial markets; the management of public assets; human capital; the business environment; decentralisation and regional policy; the energy sector; transport; the agri-food sector; digital transformation; and green transition and environmental protection.

- (9) As set out in the Annex to this Decision, the Commission has assessed Ukraine’s request in detail in accordance with Article 26(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/792. It has concluded that Ukraine has satisfactorily fulfilled step 4.3 under the fourth instalment under the Plan, steps 4.8, 6.7 and 10.3 under the seventh instalment, step 15.8 under the eighth instalment, and steps 3.10, 3.17, 4.20, 5.3, 5.5, 7.7, 7.12, 7.14, 7.18, 7.20, 8.12, 9.7, 10.19, 12.9, 14.4 and 15.11 under the ninth instalment.

- (10) The Commission has assessed that Ukraine partially fulfilled step 6.3 under the ninth instalment. In addition, the Commission has assessed that Ukraine has satisfactorily fulfilled step 10.21 under the tenth instalment. Therefore, in line with the methodology for handling partial fulfilment of steps in the Plan set out in the Communication from the Commission of 17 April 2026 [^ntr6-L_202602596EN.000101-E0006], the Commission proposes to take this into account as a mitigating factor.

- (11) Furthermore, the Commission has assessed that Ukraine continues to fulfil the pre-condition for Union support set out in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792. In particular, Ukraine continues to uphold and respect effective democratic mechanisms, including a multi-party parliamentary system and the rule of law, and to guarantee respect for human rights, including the rights of persons belonging to minorities.

- (12) In its payment request, Ukraine confirmed that it had not reversed any measure related to the steps which it had previously satisfactorily fulfilled.

- (13) This Decision should therefore establish that Ukraine has satisfactorily fulfilled the relevant conditions for the payment of the fourth instalment in respect of one outstanding step under the Plan, of the seventh instalment in respect of three outstanding steps, of the eighth instalment in respect of one outstanding step and of the ninth instalment in respect of sixteen steps.

- (14) Considering Ukraine’s urgent financing needs, it is of the utmost importance to disburse the funds as soon as possible. With a view to expediting the process, this Decision should enter into force on the date of its publication in the *Official Journal of the European Union* and should apply from the date of its adoption,

HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION:

#### Article 1

1. In accordance with the Commission’s assessment provided pursuant to Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792, set out in the Annex to this Decision, the Council establishes that Ukraine has satisfactorily fulfilled the relevant conditions for the partial payment pursuant to Implementing Decision (EU) 2024/1447 of the fourth, seventh, eighth and ninth instalments of financial assistance under the Ukraine Plan. Such assistance shall amount to EUR 8 040 665 425 before clearing the pre-financing, of which EUR 298 747 120 corresponds to the fourth instalment, EUR 368 010 368 corresponds to the seventh instalment, EUR 95 530 669 corresponds to the eighth instalment, and EUR 7 278 377 268 corresponds to the ninth instalment.

2. Taking into account the satisfactory fulfilment of one step under the tenth instalment and the partial fulfilment of one step under the ninth instalment, the importance of the steps that have not been fulfilled, the reasons for their non-fulfilment, and the importance of the step that has been fulfilled early, a mitigating factor amounting to EUR 529 397 883 is applied, thereby reducing the suspension value corresponding to the unfulfilled steps under the ninth instalment.

#### Article 2

This Decision shall enter into force on the date of its publication in the *Official Journal of the European Union*.

It shall apply from the date of its adoption.

Done at Brussels, 16 November 2026.

***For the Council***

***The President***

**K. KALLAS**

## ANNEX

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On 30 September 2026, Ukraine submitted a request for payment of part of the fourth, seventh, eighth and ninth instalments of the Ukraine Plan, in accordance with Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2024/792 of 29 February 2024 establishing the Ukraine Facility [^ntr1-L_202602596EN.000401-E0001]. To support the payment request, Ukraine provided justification for the satisfactory fulfilment of one step in the fourth instalment, three steps in the seventh instalment, one step in the eighth instalment and 17 steps in the ninth instalment, set out in the Annex to Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2024/1447 of 14 May 2024 on the approval of the assessment of the Ukraine Plan [^ntr2-L_202602596EN.000401-E0002], as subsequently amended (‘the CID Annex’). Ukraine also provided evidence for one step in the 10th instalment.

On the basis of the information provided by Ukraine, 16 steps of the ninth instalment are considered to have been satisfactorily fulfilled.

- — Step 3.10, concerning the upgraded IT system for enforcement of court decisions is operational.

- — Step 3.17, concerning the adoption of the Presidential Decree approving the Human Rights Protection Strategy.

- — Step 4.20, concerning the adoption of the State Programme to combat human trafficking until 2030.

- — Step 5.3, concerning the adoption of the strategy for gradual reduction of state ownership in the banking sector.

- — Step 5.5, concerning the entry into force of legal acts to improve resolution of non-performing loans.

- — Step 7.7, concerning the adoption of the Population Employment Strategy.

- — Step 7.12, concerning investments of at least EUR 300 million in education.

- — Step 7.14, concerning investments of at least EUR 200 million in healthcare.

- — Step 7.18, concerning investments of at least EUR 200 million for providing housing for veterans with disabilities of the I-II groups, family members of deceased veterans and internally displaced persons.

- — Step 7.20, concerning the approval by the Cabinet of Ministers of the Operational Plan of Measures for the implementation in 2026-2028 of the National Strategy for Overcoming the Gender Pay Gap for the period up to 2030.

- — Step 8.12, concerning the adoption of amendments to the resolution on the designation of the single national metrology institute.

- — Step 9.7, concerning the allocation of the equivalent of at least 5 % of the overall non-repayable financial support for meeting recovery, reconstruction and modernisation needs of sub-national authorities.

- — Step 10.19, concerning the preparation of the Terms of Reference for a third-party of the National Energy and Utilities Regulatory Commission regarding the integrity and robustness of its financial and decision-making processes.

- — Step 12.9, concerning investments of at least EUR 30 million in demining of agricultural land.

- — Step 14.4, concerning the entry into force of the functioning of the Integrated Electronic Identification System, in line with the principles of Regulation (EU) 2024/1183.

- — Step 15.11, concerning the adoption of amendments to the Procedure for State Water Monitoring.

The Commission has assessed that the evidence provided for two of the steps required for the ninth instalment is not sufficient to conclude that they are satisfactorily fulfilled (Step 6.3 concerning the appointment of supervisory boards of state-owned enterprises with a majority of independent members and Step 10.9 concerning the adoption of a roadmap for the gradual liberalisation of gas and electricity markets to be implemented after the expiration of martial law).

In accordance with the methodology for handling the partial fulfilment of steps under the Ukraine Plan (‘the methodology’), the progress made in the implementation of the quantitative Step 6.3 was proposed as a mitigating factor:

- — Step 6.3, concerning the appointment of supervisory boards of state-owned enterprises with a majority of independent members.

In addition, and in accordance with the methodology, the following step scheduled for the 10th instalment was declared by Ukraine as fulfilled and was also proposed as a potential mitigating factor:

- — Step 10.21, concerning the request for an integrated regulatory review service mission at SNRIU by the International Atomic Energy Agency.

Furthermore, the information provided by Ukraine, shows that one step from the fourth instalment, three steps from the seventh instalment and one step from the eighth instalment have been satisfactorily fulfilled.

- — Step 4.3, concerning increased manpower for the High Anti-Corruption Court.

- — Step 4.8, concerning the preparation and implementation of the next National Risk Assessment.

- — Step 6.7, concerning the entry into force of legislation on the separation of public service obligations (PSO) and non-PSO activities.

- — Step 10.3, concerning the entry into force of the legislation to improve permitting procedures for renewable energy investments.

- — Step 15.8, concerning the adoption of the Strategy for implementing the principles of the circular economy and its Action Plan.

As part of **Chapter 3** on the judicial system, an upgraded IT system for enforcement of court decisions became operational; and the Presidential Decree approving the Human Rights Protection Strategy was adopted.

As part of **Chapter 4** on the fight against corruption and money laundering, the manpower for the High Anti-Corruption Court was increased, the next National Risk Assessment was conducted and the State Programme to combat human trafficking until 2030 was adopted.

As part of **Chapter 5** on financial markets, the strategy for gradual reduction of state ownership in the banking sector was adopted; and the legislation to improve the resolution of non-performing loans entered into force.

As part of **Chapter 6** on management of public assets, the legislation on the separation of public service obligations (PSO) and non-PSO activities entered into force.

As part of **Chapter 7** on human capital, the Population Employment Strategy was adopted, there have been investments of at least EUR 300 million in education and investments of at least EUR 200 million for providing housing for veterans with disabilities of the I-II groups, family members of deceased veterans and internally displaced persons. At the same time, the operational plan of measures for the implementation in 2026-2028 of the National Strategy for Overcoming the Gender Pay Gap for the period up to 2030 was approved.

As part of **Chapter 8** on the business environment, amendments to the resolution on the designation of the single national metrology institute were adopted.

As part of **Chapter 9** on the decentralisation and regional policy, the allocation of the equivalent of at least 5 % of the overall non-repayable financial support for meeting recovery, reconstruction and modernisation needs of sub-national entities was reached.

As part of **Chapter 10** on the energy sector, the legislation on shortening the permitting procedures for renewable energy investments entered into force; the Terms of Reference for a third-party audit of the National Energy and Utilities Regulatory Commission regarding the integrity and robustness of its financial and decision-making processes were prepared and the request for an integrated regulatory review service mission at SNRIU by the International Atomic Energy Agency was submitted.

As part of **Chapter 14**, on digital transformation, the legislation on the functioning of the Integrated Electronic Identification System, in line with Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, was adopted and entered into force.

As part of **Chapter 15** on green transition and environmental protection, the Strategy for implementing the principles of circular economy and its Action Plan and the legislation amending the Procedure for State Water Monitoring were adopted.

For the assessment of investment Steps 7.12, 7.14, 7.18, 9.7 and 12.9, expenses reported in Ukrainian hryvnia (UAH) were converted into euro (EUR) using the average of the monthly UAH/EUR exchange rates for the relevant budget year. For expenditure reported in 2026, the average monthly exchange rate was calculated with the latest available data as of October 2026.

The relevant amendments were adopted by NBU Resolution No 12 of 7 February 2025 and entered into force on 1 January 2026. Under the amended Regulation, banks must update their strategies for non-performing loans and their sale plans and then update them annually. Law No 3985-IX introduced the relevant amendments to the Code of Ukraine on Bankruptcy Procedures, including the framework for preventive restructuring and related bankruptcy-procedure reforms. The changes are reflected in articles covering the requirements for the arbitration manager, the rights and liabilities of the arbitration manager, the control over the activities of arbitration managers, the disciplinary penalties, the appointment and removal of the arbitration manager and the temporary suspension of the right to carry out the activities of the arbitration manager. These changes strengthen the responsibility of the arbitration manager for the performance of their duties, impose additional requirements on arbitration managers and regulate their rights and enforcement measures in the event of violations. The detailed electronic auction procedures were subsequently aligned with the amended Code through CMU Resolution No 1566 of 3 December 2025, which entered into force on 10 December 2025. The measures identified in the NPL Strategy concerning the improvement of the procedure for and access to bankruptcy and restructuring procedures for individuals, micro and small enterprises were implemented through Law No 3985-IX of 19 September 2024 and the subsequent implementing amendments to the electronic auction framework introduced by CMU Resolution No 1566 of 3 December 2025. These changes introduce more flexible and predictable rules for the sale of assets in bankruptcy proceedings. They clarify the conduct and outcome of auctions, including the treatment of guarantee fees, the obligations of the winning bidder, and the procedure to be followed where the winner refuses to complete the purchase. In such cases, the electronic trading system automatically identifies the bidder offering the next-highest price, while failed auctions are subject to a defined procedure for determining their outcome. The amendments also provide that seizures or other restrictions on bankrupt property, where properly disclosed in the auction announcement, do not prevent the auction or the completion of the sale. In addition, CMU Resolution No 865 of 2 October 2019, ‘*Issues of the Functioning of the Electronic Trading System for the Sale of Debtors’ Property in Bankruptcy Proceedings’*, has been brought into compliance with the updated Code of Ukraine on Bankruptcy Procedures. The respective amendments were introduced by CMU Resolution No 1566 of 3 December 2025. More specifically, they brought the electronic auction procedure under CMU Resolution No 865 into line with the updated Code of Ukraine on Bankruptcy Procedures by extending its application to preventive restructuring proceedings and introducing the corresponding role of the preventive restructuring administrator. The rules governing guarantee fees, the consequences of a winner’s refusal to purchase and the transfer of winning status to the participant offering the next-highest price were aligned with the revised provisions of the Code, including the automatic generation of a new auction-results protocol. The amendments also updated procedural requirements for auction announcements, participation applications, signing and publication of auction-results protocols and the sale of seized or encumbered property. Additionally, safeguards have been introduced in the Code of Ukraine on Bankruptcy Procedures to prevent fraudulent actions of participants and limit the influence of related parties of the debtor on bankruptcy proceedings. This was done by amending Articles 45 and 47 of the Code of Ukraine on Bankruptcy Procedures introduced by Law No 3985-IX of 19 September 2024. These amendments strengthen safeguards against conflicts of interest in bankruptcy proceedings by requiring creditors to disclose any interest in or relationship with the debtor when submitting claims against the debtor. It also provides that creditors with an interest in the debtor’s property do not have a deciding vote at meetings of creditors or the creditors’ committee, thereby limiting their influence over decisions in the proceedings. The legislation on debt collection and debt treatment was also improved. Namely, Law of Ukraine No 2923-IX ‘*on Compensation for Damage to and Destruction of Certain Categories of Real Estate as a Result of Hostilities’ of*23 February 2023, which entered into force on 22 May 2023, provides a framework for compensation in respect of certain damaged or destroyed real estate and thereby supports the treatment of war-affected collateral and related debt exposures. Law of Ukraine No 4340-IX ‘*on Amendments to the Final and Transitional Provisions of the Civil Code of Ukraine Regarding the Specifics of Lending and Financial Leasing During the Period of Martial Law’* of 27 March 2025, which entered into force on 10 August 2025, establishes a temporary moratorium mechanism for qualifying borrowers affected by Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine, including certain businesses whose assets and activity are affected. Law of Ukraine No 4833 - IX ‘*on Amendments to Certain Legislative Acts of Ukraine on Improving the Procedure for Enforcement of Court Decisions, Decisions of Other Bodies and Digitalisation of Certain Stages of Enforcement Proceedings*’ was adopted in the Verkhovna Rada on 7 April 2026 with certain provisions entering into force earlier, on 24 April 2026 and 23 May 2026. It will be fully in force from 23 October 2026. Together, these measures strengthen the framework for debt treatment and recovery by addressing the consequences of war-related destruction and loss of productive assets, while providing clearer rules for borrowers and creditors affected by such circumstances. Taken together, the measures address the debt collection and treatment elements identified in the NPL strategy. Lastly, regulatory conditions favouring the NPL market’s development and operation were introduced. This was implemented through Law of Ukraine No 4466 - IX of 3 June 2025 ‘o*n Factoring’,* which entered into force on 30 July 2025 and was put into effect from 30 July 2026, following the entry into force of Law of Ukraine No 4863 of 29 April 2026 ‘*on Amending the Civil Code of Ukraine’s Provisions on Relations in the Factoring Business’*. The first law establishes a new legal framework for factoring and clarifies the legal framework for the assignment and enforcement of monetary claims, while its amendments to banking and consumer credit legislation establish rules governing the transfer of certain credit claims to eligible new creditors. The second law aligns the relevant Civil Code provisions with this new framework, creating greater legal clarity and predictability for market participants. These measures are intended to facilitate the development and operation of the factoring market and create a clearer legal framework for market participants.
